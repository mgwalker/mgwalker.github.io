---
title: Digital service delivery is just service delivery.
date: 2026-08-18
summary: Digital service delivery contains one word more than it really needs, but it has historically been an important word. That notwithstanding, the point is not digital at all.
---

Digital service delivery is a bit of a misnomer. It implies that digital
services are somehow different from services rendered via other pathways.
But that’s not true. A service cannot be delivered well digitally if it is
not delivered well anywhere else, too. Service delivery is holistic.

As a friend put it in regard to digital humanities,

> [...] if digital methodology stops being new / novel and is simply an
> expected part of how you do your work, then maybe you’re just doing
> ‘humanities.’”
> Replace “humanities” with “service delivery,” and it is no less true.

But the word “digital” has been a useful tool for getting service designers
of all stripes into rooms they otherwise would have been barred from.
Especially in a government context, where the professional staff often
feel overwhelmed with their normal duties and don’t have the time to
become experts at the digital part of delivery, offering expertise in
that specific slice is a good foot in the door.

But digital service delivery is just service delivery. As a result, the
digital service team’s purpose is not just to ship flashy websites,
time-saving automations, and rearchitected data flows. It is much
bigger. And indeed, nothing is inherently out of scope for a delivery
team. While we don’t have the authority or power to change everything,
our charter must include permission to examine, consider, and comment
on everything we see. Office leadership directs us to certain services
to focus on, but even then, as we explore how those services are
delivered to constituents, our scope may grow to identify external
constraints.

This is how every successful digital service team has always operated.
“Digital” has been regarded as a mystical “other” that offices don’t
really understand, so they hire experts to come “do digital.” The good
experts then help the office see ways that their current environment
and processes impede good delivery outcomes, though sometimes disguised
under technical jargon so the office doesn’t realize what’s really
happening. But the delivery team knows what they’re doing, and they’re
doing it with care and intent.

(A good digital service team does what an org change consultancy claims
to do. The key difference is that the digital service team actually
**_MUST_** do the organizational change part in order to deliver on
their apparent mission, whereas the org change consultancy only has
to write about it.)

---

Kelly Taylor wrote much the same thing after Colorado directed that
its statewide Office of IT (OIT) would immediately adopt the practices
of the Colorado Digital Service, which would be absorbed into the OIT,
in an essay titled
[The Digital Service Was Never the Point](https://www.eatingpolicy.com/p/the-digital-service-was-never-the)
which was published on Jen Pahlka’s website.
